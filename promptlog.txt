# Art Sandbox 01 — Luminous Probability Pavilion

## Context

Create a standalone interactive 3D art prototype for **Market Under Stress**, a quantitative visualization experience.

The current task is solely about art production: 3D geometry, environmental composition, materials, lighting, luminous effects, and tactile visual interaction.

The selected art direction combines:

- **Luminous Art Installation (primary)**
- **Floating Kinetic Sculpture (secondary)**
- **Playful Low-Poly Spatial World (environment)**

Do not recreate an arcade cabinet or traditional pinball machine.

## 1. Sandbox isolation

The workspace selected in Codex Desktop is the sandbox.

Verify the working directory before executing any commands.

Create all necessary files inside this workspace only.

Do not access or modify the existing Market Under Stress application repository. Do not set up merging, synchronization, or shared dependencies with it.

Do not initialize a remote Git repository or deploy this sandbox.

## 2. Objective

Build a locally runnable Three.js art prototype that can be visually inspected from several camera angles.

Prioritize making an attractive, distinctive, spatial composition rather than a technical demo.

Use Vite, TypeScript, and Three.js. Keep dependencies minimal.

## 3. Main installation

Create a large, freestanding **Luminous Probability Pavilion**.

The installation should have:

- An elegant open architectural frame
- Curved or gently asymmetric low-poly structural elements
- Visible depth, thickness, and soft bevels
- Suspended structural rings and floating sculptural elements
- A recognizable staggered peg field
- A luminous emitter above the peg field
- A sculptural result collection area below
- Two integrated abstract physical controls

For this visual prototype, use 16 rows of pegs and 17 collection bins.

The frame must not obstruct the mathematical visual area.

Avoid arcade consoles, enclosed cabinets, industrial machinery, dashboard screens, and casino imagery.

Aim for a visually distinctive object with a strong silhouette from both front and three-quarter views.

## 4. Spatial environment

Place the installation in a small stylized low-poly world.

Create a broad sculptural ground plane with soft variation, a carefully shaped raised platform, and a sense of depth.

The environment should extend behind and around the pavilion.

Add subtle floating architecture or abstract structures in the background, but do not clutter the primary composition.

Leave sufficient space behind the pavilion for a future mathematical density terrain.

A simple abstract background silhouette is acceptable. Do not build the actual terrain or its mathematical surface.

The sculpture should feel like part of a physical world, not an isolated model inside a rectangular UI card.

## 5. Material design

Create a centralized material palette.

Use these starting colors:

| Role | Color |
|---|---|
| Dusk Plum environment | #29233B |
| Warm Ceramic structure | #E9DCCD |
| Sage structural accent | #8CA8A0 |
| Upward movement | #FFAD78 |
| Downward movement | #6DCEEF |
| Probability density | #FFF5E8 |
| Selected object | #C5ACFF |

Physical structures should have matte or ceramic-like materials, with moderately high roughness and low metallic values.

Use color blocking to give the world personality.

Avoid overly realistic worn textures, chrome surfaces, holographic panels, and neon outlines.

**Matter is matte. Data emits light.**

## 6. Luminous effects

Create subtle but attractive light-emitting elements:

- A glowing emission core
- A few glowing demonstration particles
- Soft directional trails
- Small highlights at meaningful contact points
- A restrained luminous result area

Use bloom through the Three.js postprocessing system if appropriate.

Ensure bloom can be enabled or disabled for comparison.

Bloom should primarily emphasize luminous elements, not the entire architectural structure.

Preserve the difference between matte geometry and bright data.

## 7. Lighting presets

Implement two switchable lighting configurations.

### Soft Gallery

- Clear visibility of the sculpture
- Soft warm key lighting
- Gentle cool fill lighting
- Readable low-poly forms
- Subtle shadows and material definition

### Luminous Dusk

- Atmospheric plum background
- More pronounced luminous details
- Gentle rim lighting
- Clearly visible trajectories
- Stronger contrast without crushing the structural details

An optional 1K Studio Small 08 HDRI may be loaded from `public/env/` if supplied.

If it is absent, use procedural lighting or Three.js RoomEnvironment. Do not make external asset downloads a prerequisite for running the sandbox.

## 8. Sculptural controls

Create two distinctive physical controls integrated into the installation.

### Mood

A rotating arc or sculptural balance dial.

It should have a clear center position and respond visibly when manipulated.

### Yesterday

A curved sliding rail or suspended kinetic ring expressing inertia.

It should feel different from Mood.

These are art interaction previews only.

Their positions can change visual pose and lighting, but they must not generate financial values.

Provide basic pointer interaction and a simple accessible keyboard or UI alternative.

Do not use arcade push buttons.

## 9. Motion preview

Create several deterministic demonstration ball trajectories moving through the peg field.

Use fixed predefined direction sequences.

These are visual placeholders, not actual market or Galton simulation results.

- Warm means an upward/right step
- Cool means a downward/left step
- Trails should be short and soft
- Movement should feel light, responsive, and tactile
- Use subtle easing and bounce at the pegs

Add play, pause, and restart controls for previewing the animation.

Do not display simulated returns or invent financial statistics.

## 10. Camera system

Provide three camera presets:

1. **Front** — tests readability of the peg layout and collection bins
2. **Three-quarter** — default beauty composition showing sculptural depth
3. **Elevated / Behind** — tests the pavilion and the spatial connection toward the rear environment

Allow restrained orbit and zoom interaction.

Camera movement between presets should be smooth.

Do not implement driving, character movement, or free-roam controls.

## 11. Minimal art-review interface

Include a small nonintrusive interface for:

- Camera preset
- Lighting preset
- Bloom toggle
- Animation play/pause
- Animation restart
- Reset camera

This UI is only for art review. It is not the final Market Under Stress HUD.

The 3D canvas should occupy the primary viewport.

## 13. Quality requirements

The first view should have a carefully composed visual hierarchy.

Priorities:

1. Distinctive sculptural silhouette

## One place AI got it wrong

One major issue was readability. I initially treated it as final polish, but playtesting showed that even though the math worked, the experience was hard to understand. This makes me to add another step before implemention, where I added simpler explanations, Mood/Herd controls, and captions. It was a design problem rather than a coding bug.

Another issue was AI design drift. An earlier AI session turned the project into a moving-average backtesting dashboard, losing my original three-chapter concept. I rewrote DESIGN.md and removed outdated documents to keep future AI development aligned with my vision.
